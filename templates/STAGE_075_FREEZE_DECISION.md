# Stage 7.5 — Full-Theory Freeze Decision

> Canonical authority: `GOVERNANCE.md` → `THEORY_PAPER_RESEARCH_PIPELINE.md` → this template.

## 0. Role

Act as a senior field editor deciding whether the research branch deserves full-paper investment. No extensions are allowed in this stage.

## 1. Project context

- Topic: `[RESEARCH_TOPIC]`
- Working title: `[WORKING_TITLE]`
- Core question: `[CORE_RESEARCH_QUESTION]`
- Canonical model: `[CANONICAL_MODEL]`
- Core mechanism: `[CORE_MECHANISM]`
- Surviving propositions: `[CURRENT_STAGE_RESULT]`
- Closest papers: `[CLOSEST_PAPERS]`
- Target journal: `[TARGET_JOURNAL]`
- Known blockers: `[KNOWN_BLOCKERS]`

## 2. Stage objective

Decide whether the project contains a general economic mechanism worthy of a full paper, rather than a technically correct but model-specific algebraic result.

## 3. Canonical inputs

Freeze all Stage 0–7 results. This is an evaluation stage, not a model-development stage.

## 4. Allowed changes

Only clarification, compression, and contribution reclassification.

## 5. Prohibited changes

No new variables, extensions, robustness exercises, contracts, states, applications, or theorem engineering.

## 6. Mandatory tasks

Answer explicitly:

1. Can the main mechanism be explained without notation?
2. What is the minimal causal/strategic chain?
3. Which assumption is essential for the mechanism?
4. Which assumptions are normalization or tractability devices?
5. Does the result survive at least one credible alternative formulation already tested?
6. Is the welfare or organizational implication substantive?
7. Can the closest-paper distinction be stated in one precise paragraph?
8. Would a skeptical field referee see more than a parameter exercise?
9. Is the contribution broad enough for `[TARGET_JOURNAL]`?
10. Would a research note, shorter journal, or pivot be more appropriate?

Construct a “mechanism card” with: phenomenon → friction → strategic response → equilibrium effect → welfare effect → empirical implication.

## 7. Evidence requirements

Use only verified mathematics, literature, and institutional evidence from prior stages. Do not upgrade conjectures to established propositions.

## 8. Verification protocol

Check that every proposed contribution claim maps to a verified result and a documented prior-art distinction. Identify any claim that depends on an unresolved conjecture.

## 9. Kill tests

Do not proceed to a full paper if:

- the result cannot be explained outside notation;
- the mechanism is just a specific functional-form interaction;
- novelty depends on a narrow application label;
- the closest paper difference is cosmetic;
- the welfare implication is absent or mechanical;
- the main result still has a fatal referee attack.

## 10. Success criteria

A full-paper `GO` requires a concise general mechanism, defensible novelty, verified main results, welfare or organizational relevance, and a credible journal path.

## 11. Failure criteria

Recommend `NO-GO / RESEARCH NOTE / PIVOT` when the work is correct but too narrow, derivative, fragile, or application-specific.

## 12. Required final output

1. Executive freeze-decision verdict
2. Mechanism card
3. Essential vs tractability assumptions
4. Core propositions and proof status
5. Closest-paper distinction
6. Welfare/generality case
7. Fatal/major referee risks
8. Full-paper value assessment
9. Recommended journal level
10. Exact Stage 8 freeze scope or pivot scope

## 13. Final verdict

Choose exactly one:

- `GO TO FULL PAPER`
- `CONDITIONAL GO` — name one theorem/robustness blocker only
- `NO-GO / RESEARCH NOTE / PIVOT`

## 14. Next-stage contract

If `GO`, Stage 8 freezes the theory. If `CONDITIONAL GO`, return only to the stage required to resolve the named blocker, then repeat Stage 7.5.
