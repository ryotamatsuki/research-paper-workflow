# Stage 12 — Journal Positioning

> Canonical authority: `GOVERNANCE.md` → `THEORY_PAPER_RESEARCH_PIPELINE.md` → this template.

## 0. Role

Act as an editor-level journal strategist. Match the completed contribution to current journals using fresh evidence, not prestige preference.

## 1. Project context

- Topic: `[RESEARCH_TOPIC]`
- Working title: `[WORKING_TITLE]`
- Core mechanism/results: `[CURRENT_STAGE_RESULT]`
- Closest papers: `[CLOSEST_PAPERS]`
- Candidate target: `[TARGET_JOURNAL]`
- Alternative journals: `[ALTERNATIVE_JOURNALS]`
- Current date: `[CURRENT_DATE]`

## 2. Stage objective

Choose a realistic submission sequence based on current scope, recent publications, contribution type, theoretical sophistication, empirical expectations, likely referee fit, and the actual submission obligations of the leading target. Before ranking journals, establish that the serious-candidate set is not an accidental or path-dependent shortlist.

Stage 12 creates an initial journal-requirements baseline. It does not substitute for the mandatory live re-check at Stage 14.

## 3. Canonical inputs

The manuscript and contribution set surviving Stage 11 are fixed. Journal selection follows the paper; the paper does not acquire a new mechanism to chase a journal.

## 4. Allowed changes

Journal-specific presentation, length, formatting, emphasis, and cover-letter strategy that do not alter substantive claims.

## 5. Prohibited changes

- no relying on stale journal information;
- no claiming official acceptance rates unless verified;
- no turning an economics mechanism into a managerial result merely because another outlet looks easier;
- no journal choice based only on impact factor/ranking;
- no treating a remembered rule, old internal note, prior submission experience, or generic publisher page as sufficient evidence when a current journal-specific rule is available.

## 6. Mandatory tasks

### 6.1 Construct the candidate universe before ranking

Generate a broad candidate-journal universe from the **actual Stage-11-surviving paper**, using:

- primary field and intended scholarly audience;
- contribution type and likely article form (for example full article, short paper, note, comment, correction, replication, re-examination);
- methodological type and level of sophistication;
- manuscript scale and material format constraints;
- materially relevant geographic, institutional, or interdisciplinary audience when applicable.

Do **not** generate the universe by starting from prestige, a remembered favorite journal, or the destination of a previous paper.

Next perform a **literature-venue completeness cross-check** using the closest papers carried from Stages 2/6/11:

- treat their publication venues as prompts for omission detection, not automatic targets;
- do not promote a venue merely because a close paper appeared there;
- if an obvious field journal or a venue repeatedly represented in relevant close literature is excluded, record the exclusion reason.

Conditional rule:

- for a correction, comment, replication, re-examination, or closely related source-audit paper, explicitly consider the journal of the target/source paper because of archival and audience continuity;
- do not apply this source-journal rule mechanically to ordinary original research.

Create a **Candidate-Universe Ledger** with at least:

- journal;
- discovery path;
- serious candidate: yes/no;
- inclusion or exclusion rationale;
- evidence still needed.

Only after this ledger exists should journals be ranked.

### 6.2 Inspect serious candidates

Use current web research to inspect each serious candidate:

1. current aims and scope;
2. recent related papers, preferably last 3–5 years;
3. typical theoretical sophistication and contribution type;
4. whether empirical implications/evidence are expected;
5. article format/length requirements when material;
6. current submission/disclosure/AI policies when material;
7. desk-reject and referee-fit risks;
8. closest-paper overlap within that journal's audience.

For the selected primary journal, create an initial **Journal Requirements Ledger** using `checklists/JOURNAL_REQUIREMENTS_CHECKLIST.md`. At minimum record, from current official sources where publicly available:

- article type and manuscript category;
- review/anonymity model and where author-identifying information belongs;
- title-page and corresponding-author requirements;
- accepted initial-submission file types;
- whether editable Word/LaTeX source is required at initial submission or only later;
- LaTeX source-package rules, including figures, bibliography/style/class files, and folder/archive restrictions when applicable;
- abstract, keyword, JEL, highlight, graphical-abstract, page/word, figure/table, and artwork rules;
- supplement, code/data, repository, and data-availability requirements;
- funding, competing-interest, CRediT/author-contribution, ethics, acknowledgments, and generative-AI declarations;
- prior-publication/preprint rules when relevant;
- reviewer-suggestion/opposition fields when material;
- submission fees, APC/open-access choices, or other author charges when material;
- any portal-specific instructions visible without authenticated access.

Organize candidates as appropriate into:

- `Stretch`
- `Primary`
- `Realistic fallback`
- `Safety net`

If providing acceptance probabilities, distinguish official statistics from subjective conditional estimates.

## 7. Evidence requirements

Cite current official journal pages for scope/policies and recent journal articles for fit. Label any subjective assessment as such.

For submission requirements, use the following evidence priority and record conflicts explicitly:

1. direct current instruction from the journal/editorial office applying to the actual submission;
2. current authenticated submission-portal instruction or required field;
3. current journal-specific Guide for Authors / Instructions for Authors / journal policy page;
4. current publisher-wide official guidance;
5. secondary source, prior experience, memory, or inference.

Levels 4–5 may fill gaps provisionally but may not override a more specific current source. A material requirement supported only by memory/inference must remain `UNVERIFIED` until Stage 14.

## 8. Verification protocol

Confirm that the chosen primary journal can plausibly value the actual mechanism and that no unresolved Stage 11 fatal attack is being ignored.

Run a **candidate-universe completeness audit** before closure:

- inspect whether an obvious field journal was never considered;
- inspect whether one or more venues recur in the closest-literature set but never received an evaluation or explicit exclusion;
- for correction/comment/replication/re-examination/source-audit work, verify that the target/source paper's journal was evaluated or explicitly excluded;
- verify that candidate inclusion is not simply a proxy for prestige or prior familiarity.

A strong fit for one journal does not by itself establish that the candidate universe is complete.

Confirm that the initial Journal Requirements Ledger identifies all material submission unknowns rather than silently assuming defaults. Stage 12 may carry `UNVERIFIED` items forward, but they must be explicitly listed in the Stage 13/14 contract.

## 9. Kill tests

Reject a candidate journal if:

- scope is materially misaligned;
- recent articles show a much stronger empirical/theoretical requirement the paper cannot meet;
- the paper would need a substantive new mechanism to fit;
- the closest literature there makes the novelty look especially weak.

Do not close Stage 12 if a materially plausible journal is omitted merely because it was absent from the initial shortlist. Evaluate it or record an explicit exclusion reason.

Do not declare the submission package operationally ready at Stage 12. This stage selects and maps the journal; Stage 14 must re-open and date the current submission rules before `SUBMISSION QA PASS`.

## 10. Success criteria

Produce a coherent submission ladder with a defensible primary target, pre-specified fallback logic, an auditable Candidate-Universe Ledger, a completed candidate-universe completeness audit, and an auditable initial requirements ledger for the primary journal.

## 11. Failure criteria

If no plausible field/general journal fits the contribution, recommend a lower-level or differently scoped outlet rather than overstating the paper.

If material journal requirements cannot yet be verified, preserve them as explicit `UNVERIFIED` items rather than guessing.

## 12. Required final output

1. Executive positioning recommendation
2. Candidate-Universe Ledger and discovery paths
3. Candidate-universe completeness audit, including explicit exclusions
4. Current journal evidence
5. Journal comparison table
6. Primary target and rationale
7. Stretch/fallback/safety options
8. Desk/referee risk by journal
9. Required presentation changes only
10. Initial Journal Requirements Ledger with source URLs/access dates/statuses
11. Unverified/authenticated-portal-only requirements carried forward
12. Submission sequence
13. Verdict and Stage 13 contract

## 13. Final verdict

Choose one:

- `PRIMARY JOURNAL SELECTED — GO TO INTEGRATION`
- `CONDITIONAL GO` — one journal-fit issue
- `REPOSITION / LOWER TARGET`

## 14. Next-stage contract

Stage 13 may integrate and polish the manuscript for the selected journal but may not alter the frozen theory without reopening earlier stages. Stage 13 inherits both the Candidate-Universe Ledger and the Journal Requirements Ledger. If a materially omitted journal is discovered before submission, reopen Stage 12 and rerun positioning; Stage 14 must refresh live requirements for the selected journal rather than assuming the Stage-12 snapshot is still current.
