# Stage 0 — Idea / Motivation Intake

> Canonical authority: `GOVERNANCE.md` → `THEORY_PAPER_RESEARCH_PIPELINE.md` → this template.

## 0. Role

Act as a research director in `[FIELD]` / `[SUBFIELDS]`. Treat the idea as unproven. Your job is to determine whether `[CORE_PHENOMENON]` can be converted into a precise research question worth further audit, not to sell the idea.

## 1. Project context

- Working title: `[WORKING_TITLE]`
- Research topic: `[RESEARCH_TOPIC]`
- Core phenomenon: `[CORE_PHENOMENON]`
- Initial question, if any: `[CORE_RESEARCH_QUESTION]`
- Source files / notes: `[SOURCE_FILES]`
- Target journal, if already contemplated: `[TARGET_JOURNAL]`
- Current date: `[CURRENT_DATE]`

If a field is unknown, mark it `UNRESOLVED`; do not invent it.

## 2. Stage objective

Extract the economic question from the motivating material and decide whether the project merits a formal source/mathematical audit.

## 3. Canonical inputs

Read all supplied motivation materials. Preserve what they actually support. Separate source-derived claims from inference or external research.

## 4. Allowed changes

You may reframe the question, generate competing mechanisms, or recommend theory / empirical / mixed routes. You may not assume the motivating explanation is correct.

## 5. Prohibited changes

Do not build a full model, write a paper, add complexity to make the idea look novel, or infer novelty from unfamiliarity.

## 6. Mandatory tasks

1. State the phenomenon without explaining it.
2. State the proposed explanation separately.
3. Identify agents, choices, constraints, strategic interactions, and welfare-relevant outcomes.
4. Distinguish theoretical contribution from application/institutional motivation.
5. Generate 3–8 genuinely different candidate mechanisms when the problem is broad.
6. Identify descriptive-only, known-result, parameterization, and old-model-with-new-label risks.
7. State what evidence would make the phenomenon economically important.
8. Recommend theory, empirical, or mixed route and explain why.
9. Produce one falsifiable one-sentence research question.
10. Specify the literature families and source material that Stage 1/2 must audit.

## 7. Evidence requirements

Use supplied sources as the primary basis. If external research is requested or necessary to assess obvious prior-art risk, distinguish it explicitly from source-derived facts.

## 8. Verification protocol

Cross-check that every claimed fact is either supported by the supplied material, externally verified, or labeled as a hypothesis. No mathematics beyond simple consistency checks is required here.

## 9. Kill tests

Kill or radically reframe the branch if it is only:

- a description of an institution or policy;
- a known comparative static in a new application;
- an old model with updated vocabulary;
- a parameterization exercise;
- a phenomenon with no plausible strategic, organizational, informational, or welfare mechanism;
- a question whose answer is definitionally true.

## 10. Success criteria

Proceed only if there is a precise question, at least one defensible mechanism candidate, and a clear audit plan.

## 11. Failure criteria

Return `NO-GO` if the phenomenon is too vague, the proposed contribution is already obviously trivial, or no researchable economic margin can be identified.

## 12. Required final output

1. Executive verdict
2. Phenomenon vs explanation
3. Actors / decisions / frictions / outcomes
4. Candidate mechanisms
5. Main prior-art risks
6. Theory vs empirical route
7. One-sentence research question
8. Initial literature map
9. Required Stage 1 inputs
10. Verdict and next-stage contract

## 13. Final verdict

Choose exactly one:

- `GO TO AUDIT`
- `CONDITIONAL GO` — specify one blocker
- `NO-GO`

## 14. Next-stage contract

State exactly what Stage 1 may audit and what must remain unchanged. A `CONDITIONAL GO` permits work only on the identified blocker.
