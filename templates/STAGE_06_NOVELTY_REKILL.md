# Stage 6 — Novelty Re-Kill

> Canonical authority: `GOVERNANCE.md` → `THEORY_PAPER_RESEARCH_PIPELINE.md` → this template.

## 0. Role

Act as a hostile novelty referee. Search the actual results generated by the model, not the original motivation or setup. For strategic generalizations, judge the full game against its nested benchmarks rather than treating separately known ingredients as automatic absorption.

## 2. Stage objective

Determine whether the theorems, thresholds, mode switches, sign reversals, equilibrium interactions, conditions-for-effectiveness results, or welfare wedges that survived Stages 4–5 are already present in the literature or are immediate corollaries of existing theory.

For a generalization/unification route, determine whether the **full-game-only result** is actually new, not merely whether each component is known.

## 3. Canonical inputs

The model and propositions are frozen for this gate. Do not modify them in response to prior art.

## 4. Allowed changes

You may delete, narrow, or reclassify contribution claims. You may identify a new Stage 3 pivot if all claims are killed.

## 5. Prohibited changes

- no setup-only novelty search;
- no relabeling a killed theorem;
- no ignoring close results because their application differs;
- no claiming novelty from a different functional form when the mechanism is the same;
- no declaring absorption merely because different papers separately contain the model's ingredients;
- no defending a generalization solely by saying that no paper has combined the exact ingredients.

## 6. Mandatory tasks

For each main proposition, create targeted searches using:

1. theorem language;
2. mechanism language;
3. threshold/sign-reversal language;
4. organizational transition language;
5. welfare result language;
6. game-architecture/player-objective-strategy language;
7. alternative terminology used in adjacent fields.

Check exact predecessor, structurally close theorem, same mechanism in another application, same welfare result, appendix/extension versions, and current working papers.

For strategic/game-theoretic work, re-run the whole-game absorption test:

- Can one prior model reproduce the economically relevant full game?
- Is the headline result an immediate corollary of one prior theorem?
- If multiple prior models are required to reconstruct separate components, what interaction result arises only in the full game?

For a generalization/unification route, re-open the papers corresponding to every nested benchmark and verify:

- the benchmark mapping is correct;
- the full-model proposition is unavailable in each benchmark alone;
- the claimed contribution is generated by the interaction of the generalized strategic margins rather than by notation or an ad hoc parameter.

Use `checklists/LITERATURE_AUDIT_CHECKLIST.md` and `checklists/NOVELTY_KILL_CHECKLIST.md`.

## 7. Evidence requirements

For every surviving contribution, provide a closest-paper comparison at model/proposition level and state evidentiary limits.

An absorption verdict for a strategic architecture requires direct model-level support. Component-by-component citations alone are insufficient.

## 8. Verification protocol

Re-open the strongest close papers rather than relying on Stage 2 notes. Map each candidate contribution to a specific prior-art classification.

For a generalization/unification claim, produce a result table:

| Result | Nested benchmark A | Nested benchmark B | Full model | Prior-art status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

The main contribution must be tied to a row where the full model yields a substantively different result that is not already established by a benchmark or direct prior theorem.

## 9. Kill tests

Kill a contribution when the same strategic trade-off and result are already present even if notation, industry, or timing labels differ. Downgrade combination novelty when all interaction effects are already separately known and the combination produces no new theorem.

Kill a claimed generalization when:

- it merely nests known models without generating a new strategic or welfare result;
- the full-model result is an immediate corollary of one nested benchmark;
- the additional player/strategy changes notation but not the strategic-feedback network;
- the contribution can only be defended as “nobody has combined these ingredients.”

Do not kill solely because every ingredient is separately familiar when the full architecture generates a strategically non-equivalent equilibrium problem and a result unavailable in the nested benchmarks.

## 10. Success criteria

At least one independent theorem/mechanism/welfare result must survive as more than cosmetic novelty.

For a generalization/unification route, a surviving contribution may consist of a clean general model that nests important prior models **plus** a new full-game interaction theorem, threshold/ranking/reversal, conditions characterization, or welfare wedge.

## 11. Failure criteria

Return `NO-GO` if every main result is exact prior art, structurally very close, an obvious corollary of known work, or a generalization that adds no new economics beyond its nested benchmarks.
