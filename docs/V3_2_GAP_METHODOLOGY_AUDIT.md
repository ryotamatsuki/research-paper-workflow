## B. Primary-source methodology as a decision framework
This is a comparative *interpretation of published model design* unless the original author explicitly describes a method. In particular, Athey and Acemoglu exemplify a technique; they did not mandate our workflow. Columns follow: starting point; unanswered question; changed structure; why necessary; deliberate simplification; core result; portable lesson; comparison/identification.

### Varian — How to Build an Economic Model in Your Spare Time (1997)
- Start: a concrete observation, informal puzzle or economic discussion.
- Unknown: which minimal incentives could rationalize the observed pattern.
- Structure: the smallest agent-choice-objective-constraint arrangement that captures it.
- Rationale: isolate decisive strategic incentives before full technical elaboration.
- Simplify: suppress inessential detail and favor minimal examples; revise iteratively.
- Result: a clean unexpected implication and predictions worth checking.
- Generality: parsimony allows an insight to be recognized across contexts.
- Comparison: repeatedly reject uninformative variants; later confront literature and validity.
Author text: https://people.ischool.berkeley.edu/~hal/Papers/how-OLD.pdf ; journal https://doi.org/10.1177/056943459704100201. Author expressly says the apparent sequence is idealized and actual discovery is iterative. **Adopt:** early minimal mechanism and falsifier; **do not mandate** a rigid creativity algorithm.

### Rodrik — Economics Rules / What Models Do (2015)
- Start: concrete economic context and a plurality of models.
- Unknown: which causal story and critical assumptions fit the setting.
- Structure: select among alternative models rather than claim a single universal model.
- Rationale: valid inference requires a match between crucial assumptions and institutional facts.
- Simplify: each model tells only part of the story, deliberately omitting others.
- Result: context-conditional inference and often conflicting but coherent implications.
- Generality: common analytical mechanisms may transport only when decisive assumptions do.
- Comparison: compare predictions and check critical assumptions against context; reconsider model selection dynamically.
Original author chapter: https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120379-3cb0-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b ; author/publication description: https://www.wcfia.harvard.edu/publications/economics-rules.
**Adopt:** closest model alternatives and critical-assumption ledger. **Limit:** do not require unnecessary alternatives when only one credible theory applies.

### Acemoglu, Aghion, Bursztyn & Hémous — The Environment and Directed Technical Change (2012)
- Start: climate/environment challenge under endogenous innovation.
- Unknown: whether temporary intervention can redirect innovation and permit long-run growth.
- Structure: clean/dirty inputs, directed endogenous R&D, pollution stock and policy instruments.
- Rationale: static exogenous-technology growth misses dynamic environmental and knowledge spillovers.
- Simplify: stylized aggregate sectors and tractable substitution assumptions rather than entire national institutions.
- Result: conditional temporary clean-research support and carbon pricing; policy-delay and substitution effects.
- Generality: directed technical change with endogenous lock-in plus two policy failures, not green terminology alone.
- Comparison: benchmark policy regimes, substitutability conditions and dynamic trade-offs.
Primary article: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4692283/ ; AER https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.102.1.131.
**Adopt:** only primitives that generate the stated new puzzle; **not** mandatory dynamic R&D in every green paper.

### Athey — Monotone Comparative Statics Under Uncertainty (2002)
- Start: known class of optimization/comparative-statics problems with uncertainty.
- Unknown: general primitive conditions for signed responses without solving each parametric case.
- Structure: single-crossing and log-supermodularity properties of primitives and distributions.
- Rationale: identify abstract conditions that imply monotone choices across applications.
- Simplify: no industry-specific narrative, detailed calibration or single special functional form.
- Result: general conditions for comparative-static results in stochastic optimization.
- Generality: transferable theorem across portfolios, auctions and demand families.
- Comparison: assumptions/necessity-sufficiency arguments against narrower specific solutions.
Primary author repository: https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-8461-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b ; article DOI https://doi.org/10.1162/003355302753399481.
**Adopt:** MODEL-FIRST may legitimately deliver general theorems without a thematic causal primitive.

### Roth — The Economist as Engineer (2002)
- Start: actual matching/market design challenges.
- Unknown: whether theoretical institutions perform with realistic rules and incentives.
- Structure: matching mechanisms and institutional constraints, studied through theory, computation and experiments.
- Rationale: plausible ideal equilibrium models may omit critical operational details.
- Simplify: focus on salient design rules and market frictions rather than all sociology of the market.
- Result: better understanding and construction of workable matching designs.
- Generality: robust design principles under truthful, incentive and market-thickness constraints.
- Comparison: existing assignment mechanisms, simulations and actual operational evidence.
Original Econometrica: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1468-0262.00335/full ; author repository: https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-ec38-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b.
**Adopt:** source-backed institutional plausibility and cross-method tests when relevant; **not** experimental evidence for every theoretical theorem.

### Jaffe, Newell & Stavins — A Tale of Two Market Failures (2005)
- Start: environmental policy, incentives for innovation.
- Unknown: how environmental externality and technology spillovers jointly affect optimal intervention.
- Structure: distinct pollution and innovation market failures and incentive interactions.
- Rationale: a single conventional externality model cannot capture both dynamic knowledge and pollution effects.
- Simplify: review/theoretical synthesis rather than complete multi-country institutional model.
- Result: argument for explicitly distinguishing innovation and environmental-policy incentives.
- Generality: two-failure structure can inform other technology/public-policy settings.
- Comparison: pollution-only versus innovation-related failures; research and policy evidence.
Author archive: https://stavins.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/tale-two-market-failures-technology-and-environmental-policy ; RFF https://www.rff.org/publications/working-papers/a-tale-of-two-market-failures-technology-and-environmental-policy/.
**Adopt:** ask which externality actually enters the headline theorem; do not assume a green subsidy identifies a green causal effect.

### Aghion, Dechezleprêtre, Hémous, Martin & Van Reenen — Carbon Taxes, Path Dependency, and Directed Technical Change (2016)
- Start: observed automotive clean-versus-dirty innovation choices.
- Unknown: how carbon incentives and prior innovation stocks redirect subsequent innovation.
- Structure: directed technical change with path dependence and empirical firm/patent responses.
- Rationale: clean/dirty innovation trajectories depend on history and induced incentives.
- Simplify: automobile-technology proxies and identifiable innovation margins rather than full climate-economy GE.
- Result: path-dependence evidence and impact of carbon/fuel incentives on innovation direction.
- Generality: endogenous technology direction and history dependence as recurring economic mechanisms.
- Comparison: clean vs dirty patenting and own/aggregate prior innovation.
Publication source: https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120379-0ea8-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b .
**Adopt:** research hypotheses may originate from validated empirical regularities; do not require an unestimated mechanism to be labeled experimentally identified.

### Morita & Okoshi — Beneficial Fiscal Competition for FDI (2025)
- Start: seemingly wasteful fiscal competition despite unchanged MNE locations.
- Unknown: when subsidies could raise infrastructure and host/non-host welfare.
- Structure: government transport infrastructure, subsequent fiscal offers, MNE location choice and Cournot output; separable infrastructure-sensitive transport costs.
- Rationale: transport infrastructure creates interjurisdictional spillovers omitted in subsidy-only bidding.
- Simplify: stylized bilateral markets and specific sequential instruments.
- Result: infrastructure can rise and fiscal competition benefit welfare under stated trade-cost conditions.
- Generality: policy interactions and spillovers can reverse conventional bidding intuitions.
- Comparison: known subsidy-location game versus infrastructure-investment stage and trade-cost threshold.
Full original article: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10797-025-09894-9 .
**Adopt:** this is a close prior for subsidy + infrastructure + threshold; cannot market that combination as automatically novel.

### Hauptmeier, Mittermaier & Rincke — Fiscal Competition over Taxes and Public Inputs (2012)
- Start: observed local governments use multiple fiscal instruments for mobile capital.
- Unknown: whether taxes and productive public input are strategically interdependent.
- Structure: two-jurisdiction tax and public-input choices and empirical spatial systems.
- Rationale: tax-only competition misses policy-composition effects.
- Simplify: stylized mobile-capital jurisdictions and spatially interrelated empirical specifications.
- Result: evidence of multidimensional fiscal policy interdependencies.
- Generality: strategic cross-instrument responses are not inherently environmental.
- Comparison: multi-instrument model, observed German jurisdiction responses, existing tax competition.
Original article: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0166046211001293 .
**Adopt:** the Green regression's generic fiscal-competition benchmark is substantive, not a straw man.

## D. Decision for the actual Green Subsidies shadow case
Existing evidence supports mathematically investigated rivalry-induced sign reversal; Stage 6 classification is DISTINCT BUT NARROW and Stage 7.5 records mu=0 competitiveness transmission and proportional rival-policy effects. **None of those facts alone decides whether an environmental externality is essential or whether an analogous non-green productivity instrument yields the reversal.** A matched and mathematically legitimate environmental-feature removal or non-green substitution would be diagnostically useful *only if* its corresponding policy shock and equilibrium objects are comparable. At present classify that specific role as UNRESOLVED, while preserving all existing valid theorem-level, equilibrium and novelty certifications as historical evidence. The correct prospective author warning is: "The finding may be a general multi-instrument fiscal-competition result with green-policy interpretation; its environmental-specific incremental role is not yet isolated." This is not a scientific rejection or an instruction to withdraw the submitted paper.
A proposed further study would map: M0 Hauptmeier-like general public input/tax competition; M1 actual Green model; M2 valid substitutions/removals of environmental target/emissions, green-versus-productivity productivity effect, and—with exact scope—mu=0 competitiveness. The M2 experiments are **NOT RUN** in this audit; their feasibility must be confirmed before inference. Do not recast observed mu=0 evidence as a missing computation or infer a successful thematic ablation.
