# Stage 00 — Motivation and Question Redesign

Historical research label: Pre-model motivation redesign from a legacy seminar paper

Canonical workflow mapping: Stage 0 — Idea / Motivation Intake, with input from Stage 1 audit

Research status: Historical motivation survived; inherited model did not

## Original phenomenon

The case began from a legacy question: why might a manufacturer maintain a costly local affiliated dealer network when more efficient large-scale retail channels exist?

The motivating example came from household-appliance distribution, but the research objective was not to preserve that institutional setting as the theory.

## Why the phenomenon is economically interesting

The puzzle is organizational rather than merely descriptive. A local dealer can appear inefficient if evaluated only by current product sales. The research question therefore asks whether the dealer creates another payoff-relevant function that changes manufacturer incentives, channel organization, or welfare.

## What was preserved

- the manufacturer-versus-channel-organization question;
- the coexistence of local and large-scale retail channels;
- the possibility that the local dealer performs functions not measured by current sales alone.

## What was not preserved

- the legacy demand system as a canonical model;
- inherited parameter interpretations;
- old comparative statics merely because they were already derived;
- the assumption that the legacy paper deserved publication after modernization.

## Modern one-sentence research question

> Why does a manufacturer maintain a costly local dealer network when more efficient large-scale retail channels exist, and can the local dealer have an economically distinct service-infrastructure role that survives its retail function?

## Initial candidate mechanism

The first candidate was cross-channel service spillover: service supplied by local dealer `L` can create value not only for `L`'s own customers but also for customers buying the same brand through mass retailer `R`.

The intended conceptual separation was:

- `γ`: substitutability / intrabrand product-market competition;
- `β`: brand-wide service effect benefiting demand across channels;
- `δ`: local-channel-specific service effect.

This separation was designed to avoid letting a single parameter simultaneously represent competition, market expansion, and service value.

## Initial prior-art risks

Before claiming novelty, the project identified mature neighboring literatures in vertical restraints, retail service, after-sales service, O2O/service sharing, and channel coordination. The case therefore treated service spillover as a candidate mechanism requiring a kill test, not as a contribution.
