# Stage 02 — Static Service Contract

Historical research label: Stage 2 — Service-contract repair and novelty re-kill

Canonical workflow mapping: Stage 5 — Mechanism Hardening followed immediately by Stage 6 — Novelty Re-Kill

Research status: `NO-GO`

## Diagnosed Stage 1 failure

In the static baseline, service was financed only from local product sales. The key structure was

`e* = (ell/k) q_L*`,

so local retail exit mechanically implied service exit.

## Allowed change

Only one margin was changed: permit sales-independent service compensation / service contracting.

Everything else was kept conceptually frozen. No dynamics, installed base, additional channels, or manufacturer competition were introduced.

## Static service-only result

With a separate service contract, the state

`q_L = 0,  e > 0`

can be supported in principle.

This fixed the feasibility problem identified in Stage 1.

Evidence status: `REPORTED / REQUIRES REPRODUCTION`. The result is straightforward under service compensation, but this worked case does not archive a production model or verification script.

## Novelty Gate

The repair immediately created a new problem. In a static model with no payoff-relevant history, a former dealer that no longer sells and only supplies service is economically equivalent to an ordinary third-party service provider with the same technology and contract.

Thus:

`former dealer as service-only node ≈ generic third-party service provider`.

The project judged this too close to established service-outsourcing, after-sales-service, and service-channel-choice models.

Prior research treated Li et al. (2016), Xu–Fu–Fan (2020), Lei–Li–Cheng (2024), Zhang–Lim–Ye (2025), and Basu–Basu–Ingene (2026) as important neighboring or critical papers. Bibliographic and proposition-level details must be re-verified before any publication claim.

## Critical insight

The Stage 1 feasibility problem was solved, but the economic-identity problem was not.

That distinction is the reason this branch stopped. A model can make a desired state mathematically feasible and simultaneously become less novel.

## Strongest referee attack

> The service-only regime is mechanically created by assuming a separate service contract, and the former dealer is just a service contractor.

Under the static design, this attack was treated as fatal.

## Verdict

`STAGE 2 NO-GO`.

## Next question

Why should the former dealer possess any current economic value that a fresh third-party provider does not?

The next allowed search therefore shifted from contract design to history-dependent payoff-relevant states.

## Prohibited rescue

The project did not continue adding fixed retainers, linear subsidies, cost-sharing rules, or other static transfer instruments solely to preserve the service-only interpretation.
