# Lessons for Reuse

Historical research label: Cross-case lessons extracted from the retail-service redesign

Canonical workflow mapping: Reusable interpretation of Stages 0–6 and the one-diagnosed-fix rule

Research status: Workflow lessons only; not canonical policy

## Lesson 1 — Preserve the question, not the legacy model

An old model can be mathematically repairable and still be the wrong object to preserve. The durable asset may be the economic question, not the inherited demand system, notation, or comparative statics.

In this case, the old channel model was dropped while the question — why a manufacturer might maintain a costly local dealer despite efficient large-scale retailing — was retained.

## Lesson 2 — Negative algebra can identify the missing economic margin

The Stage 1 structure

`q_L → 0 ⇒ e → 0`

was not merely an inconvenient failure. It diagnosed why the desired service-infrastructure interpretation could not exist: service was financed only by current local product sales.

A negative theorem can therefore specify the only legitimate next modification.

## Lesson 3 — Fixing feasibility can kill novelty

A separate service contract made `q_L=0, e>0` feasible. But the repair converted the former dealer into a generic service contractor in a static model.

The correct lesson was not `the model now works`; it was `the feasibility problem is solved, but the novelty problem is worse`.

## Lesson 4 — A new state variable is not automatically a new mechanism

Installed base `I_t` explained why service demand can persist after current sales disappear. It did not explain why a former dealer should provide that service rather than a fresh third party.

Adding dynamics is not enough. Ask which decision or organizational identity the state actually changes.

## Lesson 5 — Ask why history changes identity

If a paper says a former partner, legacy retailer, incumbent contractor, or historical organization matters, history must create a current payoff-relevant difference.

In this case that candidate difference became manufacturer-specific service capability `K_t`.

## Lesson 6 — Distinguish a known component from a novel interaction

Installed-base economics may be known. Relationship-specific investment may also be known. Their coexistence is not automatically novelty.

The potentially interesting object is the interaction that changes a strategic decision — here, current vertical treatment of a dealer because current activity may create future service capability for an installed base.

## Lesson 7 — Do not call state dependence hysteresis

Different current choices at different values of inherited states establish state/history dependence. They do not by themselves establish hysteresis.

Use `hysteresis` only after the model contains and proves the relevant irreversibility or distinct transition structure.

## Lesson 8 — The best surviving result may not be the original desired result

The original target was close to: `can service survive when local sales become small?`

The more promising surviving candidate became: `can a manufacturer favor a local dealer today to create relationship-specific service capability valuable tomorrow?`

A workflow should permit the strongest surviving mechanism to replace the original hoped-for theorem.

## Lesson 9 — One diagnosed fix at a time works

The research path was:

```text
sales-margin-only finance
        ↓ diagnosed failure
separate service contract
        ↓ novelty failure
installed base
        ↓ identity failure
legacy service capital
        ↓ unresolved microfoundation
CONDITIONAL GO
```

Each step addressed the failure immediately preceding it. This made it possible to identify which assumption created which result.

## Lesson 10 — `CONDITIONAL GO` is not `GO`

The surviving dynamic branch still has a concrete blocker: the accumulation of legacy service capital is insufficiently microfounded and must be independently reproduced after redesign.

A promising mechanism is not permission to start manuscript construction. The project remains before canonical Stage 7.5.

## Reuse test

The lessons above should remain meaningful if the appliance/dealer labels are removed. They apply whenever a research project must decide whether a historical organization has current economic value, whether a failed minimal model identifies a missing margin, and whether a repair creates a new prior-art problem.

The case-specific details remain evidence about this research path; the general lesson is the method of killing and narrowing branches.
