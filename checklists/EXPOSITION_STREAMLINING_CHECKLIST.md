# Exposition Streamlining Checklist

Use across the exposition lifecycle:

- **Stage 7:** identify candidate exposition vehicles;
- **Stage 10:** design the exposition architecture before finalizing the Introduction;
- **Stage 13:** aggressively streamline the integrated manuscript;
- **Stage 14:** verify that the final package still satisfies the architecture without performing a late substantive rewrite.

This checklist generalizes a discussant-style exposition discipline associated with Michi Igami's advice on making economics papers shorter, faster to parse, and more selective about what enters the main text. The paper-specific page milestones that motivated this refinement are **not universal rules**. Journal instructions, article type, contribution type, and the economics of the paper control.

The governing rule is:

> Get the reader to the economic question, model/evidence, and main result as early as the paper permits; keep sections functionally pure; make every main-text exhibit earn its place; and delete material that does not materially improve understanding or verification of the contribution.

## 1. Scope and profile selection

Record one primary exposition profile:

- [ ] **GENERAL THEORY / THEORY-FIRST**
- [ ] **STRUCTURAL / QUANTITATIVE / EMPIRICAL**
- [ ] **CORRECTION / COMMENT / REASSESSMENT**
- [ ] **OTHER — EXPLAIN**

Also record:

- target journal/article type if known;
- current official page/word/exhibit constraints if known;
- whether the manuscript is intended as a full-length article, short paper/note, correction/comment, or another format;
- whether journal-specific requirements override any generic heuristic below.

Page counts and section-arrival milestones are diagnostics, not theorem-like constraints. A manuscript does not fail merely because it exceeds a generic benchmark; it fails when its length or ordering imposes avoidable reader cost or violates the actual journal/article-type contract.

## 2. Introduction compression test

The Introduction should answer, with minimal search cost:

- [ ] **Question:** What is the precise economic question?
- [ ] **Why care:** Why does the answer matter economically?
- [ ] **Why this setting/model:** Why is the chosen industry, institution, model class, or source paper an informative way to answer the question?
- [ ] **Finding:** What is the main result?
- [ ] **How obtained:** What is the essential method — model, data, identification, estimation, formal proof, correction, or computational method?
- [ ] **Novelty:** Relative to the few closest papers, what changes?
- [ ] **Scope:** Is the contribution described no more broadly than the certified theorem/evidence supports?

Compression diagnostics:

- [ ] The reader does not need to wait through a long literature survey before learning the question and main finding.
- [ ] The Introduction does not reproduce derivations, institutional detail, or robustness discussion that belongs later.
- [ ] Contribution claims are stated once clearly rather than repeated in slightly different forms.
- [ ] The closest-paper comparison is selective and decision-relevant.
- [ ] The Introduction's length is proportionate to the article type and target journal.

Soft heuristic: for a conventional full-length economics paper, an Introduction materially longer than roughly five manuscript pages requires an explicit reason. This is **not** a hard limit and is normally too generous for short notes, corrections, and comments.

## 3. Section-purity test

Each main section must have a dominant function. Avoid forcing the reader to assemble one object from multiple sections.

### Background / institutional context

- [ ] Contains facts, institutional context, or chronology needed to understand the question.
- [ ] Does not hide model assumptions inside descriptive prose.
- [ ] Does not contain unmotivated regression/model results that belong later.
- [ ] Distinguishes external facts from modeling choices.

### Data / evidence description, where applicable

- [ ] Sources, sample construction, variables, measurement, and purely descriptive evidence are clear.
- [ ] Descriptive facts are separated from identification/estimation unless a combined structure is explicitly justified.
- [ ] Main-text descriptive exhibits are limited to facts the reader needs later.

### Model / theoretical framework

- [ ] All economically material primitives, timing, information, strategies, payoffs, constraints, equilibrium concepts, and benchmark definitions needed for headline results are consolidated here or clearly cross-referenced.
- [ ] Static, dynamic, fee/contract, participation, and other model components are not scattered across unrelated sections.
- [ ] The core model is self-contained enough that the reader does not need to mine the Background or Appendix for missing assumptions.
- [ ] Technical regularity assumptions may be deferred only when doing so does not obscure the economic object or theorem scope.

### Identification / estimation, where applicable

- [ ] The method receives space proportional to its novelty.
- [ ] A standard method is not expanded merely because it is technically available.
- [ ] A genuinely new method receives enough detail to be evaluated and reproduced.

### Results / estimates

- [ ] The section foregrounds results used later in the paper.
- [ ] Secondary estimates or variants that do not affect the argument are moved to the Appendix/Supplement or removed.
- [ ] Tables/figures do not duplicate prose or each other.

### Simulations / counterfactuals / numerical illustrations

- [ ] Each exercise has an explicit economic question or decision role.
- [ ] The exercise is not included merely because the code can produce it.
- [ ] The reader is told what uncertainty, mechanism, welfare comparison, or policy counterfactual the exercise resolves.
- [ ] Numerical illustration is not presented as proof of a theorem.

### Conclusion

- [ ] Short.
- [ ] Answers the research question.
- [ ] States scope/limitations where useful.
- [ ] Introduces no new theorem, empirical result, policy claim, literature claim, or robustness result.

## 4. Reader-arrival budget

Instead of universal page numbers, define manuscript-specific arrival targets.

Record the page/section by which a competent reader should have encountered:

1. the research question and why it matters;
2. the main finding;
3. the core primitives/data needed to understand the result;
4. the full core model or identification logic;
5. the first headline result;
6. the quantitative/robustness/counterfactual material, where applicable.

Then audit:

- [ ] No milestone is delayed by material that can be moved later without loss.
- [ ] The paper reaches the central economic object before reader attention is consumed by secondary detail.
- [ ] The ordering reflects dependency: facts/primitives before claims that require them, but not every detail before the main result.
- [ ] Appendix placement is used to preserve auditability without imposing unnecessary main-text latency.

Profile guidance:

- **Theory-first:** model and first headline proposition should appear early; literature/background should not postpone the mechanism.
- **Structural/quantitative/empirical:** data and institutional facts should arrive early enough to motivate the model; estimation machinery should not dominate unless methodologically novel.
- **Correction/comment/reassessment:** identify the source claim, defect/question, corrected statement, and consequence as early as possible; background should be minimal and source-specific.

## 5. Main-text necessity test

For every section, subsection, paragraph cluster, theorem, example, robustness exercise, figure, and table, ask:

> If this item were removed from the main text, would a careful reader lose a material part of the question, mechanism, proof/evidence, result, scope, or reproducibility needed to evaluate the contribution?

Classify each material item:

- CORE — removal would materially damage understanding or evaluation;
- HELPFUL — useful but not essential;
- APPENDIX — should remain auditable but not in the main flow;
- DELETE — redundant, decorative, weakly motivated, or superseded.

Rules:

- [ ] Every main-text item is CORE or has a documented reason to remain HELPFUL.
- [ ] APPENDIX items are moved rather than retained from inertia.
- [ ] DELETE items are removed from both prose and cross-references.
- [ ] Compression does not remove assumptions, scope conditions, caveats, or evidence needed to avoid overclaiming.

## 6. Exhibit-scarcity test

Treat main-text exhibits as scarce resources.

For every figure/table:

- [ ] State the single question the exhibit answers.
- [ ] State what the reader learns that is materially harder to learn from a theorem, one sentence, or another exhibit.
- [ ] Confirm it is referenced and interpreted in the text.
- [ ] Confirm it does not duplicate another exhibit.
- [ ] Confirm its scale, sign, units, domain, and uncertainty are economically meaningful.
- [ ] Confirm it maps to verified source/generator provenance.

### Five-exhibit thought experiment

For a full-length paper, answer:

> If the journal allowed only five main-text exhibits total, which five would remain?

This is a prioritization device, **not a universal five-exhibit limit**.

Record:

1. Exhibit 1: why indispensable
2. Exhibit 2: why indispensable
3. Exhibit 3: why indispensable
4. Exhibit 4: why indispensable
5. Exhibit 5: why indispensable

Then:

- [ ] Every exhibit outside the top five has been challenged for Appendix relocation, merger, or deletion.
- [ ] A short note/correction uses an even stricter scarcity test appropriate to its scale.
- [ ] A paper that genuinely requires more than five exhibits records why each additional main-text exhibit changes reader inference rather than merely adding completeness.

## 7. Redundancy and repetition audit

- [ ] The same contribution is not restated in Abstract, Introduction, Results, Discussion, and Conclusion with unnecessary variation.
- [ ] Mathematical intuition is not repeated after already being made clear.
- [ ] Literature review does not reappear in Discussion.
- [ ] Institutional facts are stated once in the natural location and cross-referenced.
- [ ] Robustness discussion does not repeat baseline derivation.
- [ ] Captions do not reproduce whole paragraphs from the text.
- [ ] Appendix material is not duplicated in the main text.

## 8. Compression-without-damage test

After streamlining, verify that no deletion caused:

- [ ] a missing model primitive or assumption;
- [ ] an unexplained notation jump;
- [ ] a theorem whose domain/quantifiers are no longer visible;
- [ ] a claim detached from evidence;
- [ ] a robustness qualifier disappearing from the main claim;
- [ ] a figure/table that can no longer be interpreted independently;
- [ ] a literature claim losing its source;
- [ ] a correction/reassessment losing the exact link to the source statement being corrected;
- [ ] a formal-verification claim becoming broader than the formal artifact.

Streamlining must reduce reader cost, not evidentiary quality.

## 9. Article-type profiles

### GENERAL THEORY / THEORY-FIRST

Default emphasis:

- reach the model and main theorem quickly;
- keep institutional motivation concise unless it carries the mechanism;
- consolidate primitives and theorem scope;
- move routine algebra, long case enumeration, and secondary robustness to appendices;
- use figures/tables only when they materially explain mechanism, comparative statics, equilibrium regions, or welfare.

### STRUCTURAL / QUANTITATIVE / EMPIRICAL

Default emphasis:

- establish setting/data early;
- keep descriptive analysis descriptive;
- separate model from background facts;
- compress standard estimation machinery;
- foreground estimates that matter for later counterfactuals;
- motivate every simulation/counterfactual;
- challenge large exhibit inventories.

Paper-specific page landmarks such as "data by page 8–9," "model by page 10–13," "estimates by page 20–22," or "simulations by page 25–27" may be used only as **diagnostic examples** for a conventional long paper, never as universal pass/fail thresholds.

### CORRECTION / COMMENT / REASSESSMENT

Default emphasis:

- identify the source proposition/equation/result immediately;
- state the defect, corrected result, and substantive consequence early;
- minimize generic literature/background;
- reproduce only source material required for verification;
- keep proofs complete enough for audit but move routine expansions to an appendix when safe;
- use exhibits sparingly;
- avoid inflating a correction into a full-paper narrative when the contribution is narrow.

## 10. Stage-specific obligations

### Stage 7 — candidate vehicles

Record possible theorem/proposition/figure/table/numerical/prose vehicles for each headline result. Do not create visuals merely to satisfy a quota.

### Stage 10 — design gate

Before finalizing the Introduction:

- [ ] select the exposition profile;
- [ ] create a section-role map;
- [ ] define reader-arrival targets;
- [ ] classify headline-result vehicles;
- [ ] run the five-exhibit thought experiment;
- [ ] identify obvious Appendix/DELETE candidates;
- [ ] record unresolved exposition blockers.

Recommended artifact: EXPOSITION_ARCHITECTURE.md.

A Stage-10 exposition failure blocks progression until the architecture is repaired.

### Stage 13 — streamlining gate

On the integrated manuscript:

- [ ] classify each major section/subsection/exhibit as CORE, HELPFUL, APPENDIX, or DELETE;
- [ ] run the Introduction compression test;
- [ ] run the section-purity test;
- [ ] rerun the reader-arrival budget using actual page numbers;
- [ ] rerun the five-exhibit thought experiment;
- [ ] remove/relocate redundant material;
- [ ] verify compression did not damage theorem/evidence scope.

Recommended artifact: EXPOSITION_STREAMLINING_REPORT.md.

If the fix requires a new substantive result, model change, theorem change, empirical exercise, or novelty claim, roll back to the earliest affected research stage rather than hiding the change inside Stage 13.

### Stage 14 — verification gate

Stage 14 is a binary audit of the already integrated architecture:

- [ ] the Stage-13 streamlining report exists or equivalent evidence is preserved;
- [ ] no avoidable exposition regression was introduced by journal formatting;
- [ ] page/word/exhibit limits are satisfied or a current official exception/format explains the difference;
- [ ] the final PDF still passes Introduction, section-purity, reader-arrival, exhibit-scarcity, and conclusion checks;
- [ ] no late substantive rewrite was used to make the package fit.

A structural exposition failure at Stage 14 normally reopens Stage 13. A design failure may reopen Stage 10. A substantive claim/theory/evidence failure reopens the earliest affected stage.

## 11. Passing standard

The exposition lifecycle passes when:

- the paper states its question, importance, finding, method, and novelty with low reader search cost;
- sections are functionally coherent;
- the core model/evidence is not scattered;
- the manuscript reaches headline results without avoidable delay;
- main-text exhibits are scarce, necessary, and verified;
- secondary material is moved out of the main flow;
- the conclusion is short and does not create new claims;
- compression preserves mathematical, empirical, bibliographic, and formal-verification integrity;
- journal/article-type constraints are respected.

The objective is not maximum brevity. The objective is **minimum reader cost subject to complete and accurate scientific communication**.
